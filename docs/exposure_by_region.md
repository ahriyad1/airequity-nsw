# Threshold crossing rates by station and region

PM2.5 exceedances above 25 ug/m3, 2023-2024, 18 operational Sydney stations.

Highest crossing rates:
| Station | Region | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect | North-west | 2.59% |
| Penrith | North-west | 2.10% |
| Liverpool | South-west | 1.96% |
| Rouse Hill | North-west | 1.75% |
| Richmond | North-west | 1.62% |

Lowest crossing rates:
| Station | Region | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Randwick | East | 0.49% |
| Cook and Phillip | East | 0.53% |
| Rozelle | East | 0.68% |
| Macquarie Park | East | 0.70% |

Prospect records 5.3x the crossing rate of Randwick. The five highest-rate
stations are all in western Sydney; four of the five lowest are in Sydney East.

Crossings also concentrate in evening hours. The highest station-hour rate is
Liverpool at 21:00 (8.4%), against a network mean of 1.5%, consistent with
wood heater emissions and overnight temperature inversions.

Implication: exposure is unevenly distributed across the basin, and is highest
in the regions with sparser monitoring coverage. This strengthens the equity
motivation stated in A1 and sharpens the LOSO research question - whether
forecast reliability degrades most where event rates are highest.
